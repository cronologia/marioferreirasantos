Mário Ferreira dos Santos — Chronology

Chronology of Mário Ferreira dos Santos (1907–1968): life, works and reception

An open, source-referenced chronology of Mário Ferreira dos Santos — Brazilian philosopher, translator, and publisher of his own books; author of the unfinished Enciclopédia das Ciências Filosóficas e Sociais, and a documented presence in the anarchist Centro de Cultura Social in São Paulo.

About

Work-in-progress dataset compiled from public sources (see references). Three cautions govern almost every entry here. First, the work list: the fullest catalogue reached is published by the present-day, admirer-run Instituto Cultural Logos and derives from the philosopher's daughters — a family and admirer source, labeled as such wherever it is cited, and never turned into a neutral bibliography in this site's own voice. Second, the years: he self-published and re-edited constantly, and the catalogue and the Portuguese Wikipedia disagree about the first-edition year of at least five titles — on Filosofia Concreta the admirer site contradicts its own catalogue page. Each event records the earliest edition it can and flags the disagreement rather than choosing between them. Third, the size of the Enciclopédia das Ciências Filosóficas e Sociais is contested, and every figure on this site is attributed to whoever gave it: 52 (Olavo de Carvalho), 45 (English Wikipedia), “cerca de 50” (Moura & Azevêdo), and a catalogue numbering I–XLVIII that includes unpublished manuscripts and fragments. No source reached by this project supports the “100+ volume plan” that circulates online, and this site does not assert one. The establishment reaction closest to a contemporary verdict — Luís Washington Vita's dismissive 1953 review and his tepid 1968 obituary — is known here only through quotation in the secondary literature; the Revista Brasileira de Filosofia originals were not reached, so what those two texts actually say beyond the quoted lines is unchecked. Several sources resisted when the research was done on 12 August 2026: the É Realizações site, Vide Editorial, academia.edu, PhilPapers and SciELO were bot-walled, the Biblioteca Nacional's digitized newspapers were geo-blocked (so the Pelotas press evidence is cited second-hand through Pachêco's annexes), the Wayback availability API returned 429 for the whole session, and olavodecarvalho.org is a hijacked domain serving spam — his best-known essay on this subject is therefore cited through the IFE repost. Three negative findings are recorded rather than glossed: there is no entry for the philosopher in the CPDOC/FGV Dicionário Histórico-Biográfico Brasileiro (searched with a positive control), no estate site is confirmed to exist, and no 2007 centenary event was found. Dates not verified against a primary source are flagged. Corrections against primary sources are welcome.

Who
Mário Ferreira dos Santos (1907–1968) — Brazilian philosopher, translator, journalist and self-publisher; author of the unfinished Enciclopédia das Ciências Filosóficas e Sociais and of the system he called “filosofia concreta”. He held no university chair, refused public posts, and sold his own books door to door on credit.[3] [1] [7]
Born
3 January 1907, Tietê, São Paulo, Brazil — while his father's itinerant theatre company was passing through the town. The family settled in Pelotas, Rio Grande do Sul, in 1909.[3] [1] [2]
Died
11 April 1968, São Paulo, aged 61; cause given as cardiovascular disease. No source consulted disagrees about the date. Both dates are fixed by Pe. Stanislavs Ladusãns's declaration of 18 December 1968, quoted in the daughters' Monografia and through it in Pachêco's dissertation.[3] [1] [2] [7] [23]
Family
Father: Francisco Dias Ferreira dos Santos, Portuguese (Porto), a godson of Camilo Castelo Branco, actor-impresario and a pioneer of cinema in Brazil; a Freemason and anticlerical who nonetheless placed his son with the Jesuits. Mother: the name is discordant in the sources — “Maria do Carmo Menezes Rabello, acreana” in Pachêco (2019) against “Maria do Carmo dos Santos” in the Portuguese Wikipedia, and this project does not choose. Married Yolanda Duro Lhullier in 1929; two daughters, Yolanda Lhullier dos Santos and Nadiejda Santos Nunes Galvão, who co-authored the 2001 Monografia about him.[3] [1]
Formation
Jesuit secondary schooling at the Gymnasio Gonzaga in Pelotas, 1916–1924 (not the Ginásio Anchieta named by some secondary accounts); then law and social sciences in Porto Alegre — 1925–1928 per Pachêco, graduating in 1930 per the Portuguese Wikipedia and the admirer-run Instituto Cultural Logos biography. The completion year is unresolved here.[3] [1] [13]
The Enciclopédia — how many volumes?
Contested, and every figure below is somebody's. 52 — Olavo de Carvalho, in the essay published in Dicta&Contradicta 3. 45 — the English Wikipedia. “Cerca de 50” — Moura & Azevêdo in Problemata (2015). I–XLVIII — the numbering of the family-derived catalogue published by the Instituto Cultural Logos, which includes unpublished manuscripts and fragments. Multi-volume titles inflate any of these counts. No source reached by this project supports the “100+ volume plan” that circulates online, and this site asserts no total of its own. He himself put the pseudonymous output nobody credited to him at around twenty volumes.[10] [2] [5] [12]
Principal works
Se a Esfinge Falasse (1946, as Dan Andersen) · Teses da Existência e da Inexistência de Deus (1946, as Charles Duclos) · Realidade do Homem (1947, as Dan Andersen) · Filosofia e Cosmovisão (1952 per the catalogue, 1954 per the Portuguese Wikipedia; first edition under the Edanee imprint) · Lógica e Dialéctica (1953 / 1954) · Curso de Oratória e Retórica (1953; twelve editions, his commercial engine) · Técnica do Discurso Moderno (1953) · O Homem que Nasceu Póstumo (1954) · Análise Dialética do Marxismo (1954) · Tratado de Simbólica (1956 / 1955) · Pitágoras e o Tema do Número (1956 / 1960) · O Homem perante o Infinito (1956) · Noologia Geral (1956) · Filosofia Concreta (1957 / 1956; 4th revised edition in three volumes, 1961) · O Um e o Múltiplo em Platão (1958) · Métodos Lógicos e Dialécticos (1959) · Convite à Filosofia, Convite à Estética, Convite à Psicologia Prática (1961) · Dicionário de Filosofia e Ciências Culturais, four volumes (1963) · Invasão Vertical dos Bárbaros (1967) · A Sabedoria dos Princípios (1967), A Sabedoria da Unidade (1968), A Sabedoria do Ser e do Nada (1968, posthumous, two volumes) · posthumous: O Apocalipse de S. João (1998), A Sabedoria das Leis Eternas (2001), Cristianismo: a religião do Homem (2003), Homens da Tarde (2019, a novel written in the 1940s). Where two years are given, the first is the family-derived catalogue and the second the Portuguese Wikipedia; the disagreement is recorded, not resolved.[12] [1] [13] [19]
Publisher of his own books
Founded Editora Logos S.A. and later Editora Matese S.A.; the 1952 first edition of Filosofia e Cosmovisão carries the imprint Edanee. He pioneered door-to-door sales on credit, and the number of copies is a witness's figure in every telling: Braga (2024) writes of “a casa do milhão”, while Jaime Cubero in a 1997 interview said more than two million. This site records both attributions and neither as a fact.[12] [7] [6]
Anarchism
From 1945 he frequented the anarchist Centro de Cultura Social in São Paulo, where he met Jaime Cubero. His militancy as a Christian anarchist, and his influence on Jaime and Chico Cubero and on Maurício Tragtenberg, are documented by Ramus (Verve, 2008) and Braga (Revista Ítaca, 2024). He refused public posts throughout his life, and taught at the Jesuit Faculdade Nossa Senhora Medianeira at the end of it.[6] [7] [3]
In the COF transcription corpus
He saturates the vaulted community transcriptions of Olavo de Carvalho's Curso Online de Filosofia: name queries hit the search result limit against the index of 12 August 2026 over 1,027 files (cof 589, olavo-video 237, transcripts 201). Two reviewed files are the entry points — COF014 (11 July 2009) and COF274 (13 December 2014), both marked revisada. A full census of the corpus's engagement with him is deferred work, and 32 indexed files are known to be incomplete, so any zero measured against this corpus is a lead rather than an absence. Nothing from the corpus is quoted verbatim on this site: the corpus's own rule requires verification against the audio first.[24]

1940s
1943 1943 ? Porto Alegre, RS, Brazil Translator for the Livraria do Globo; the Nietzsche preface — He worked as a translator for the Livraria do Globo in 1943–44. The essay “O homem que foi um campo de batalha”, written in 1943, appeared in 1945 as the preface to his translation of Nietzsche's The Will to Power. It is an essay and not a book, and it is not the 1954 book O Homem que Nasceu Póstumo, with which it is routinely confused. His documented translations include Nietzsche, Pascal, Amiel, Balzac, Aristotle, Porphyry, Plato, Hierocles, Plotinus, Duns Scotus, Goethe, Whitman, Benjamin Constant and Rudolf Rocker; no Kierkegaard translation appears in any catalogue consulted here.[3] [12] [1]
1945 1945 ? São Paulo, SP, Brazil Moves to São Paulo; the Centro de Cultura Social — He moved to São Paulo in financial difficulty and began frequenting the anarchist Centro de Cultura Social in the same year, where he met Jaime Cubero. Ramus (2008) and Braga (2024) document his militancy as a Christian anarchist and his influence on Jaime and Chico Cubero and on Maurício Tragtenberg. Olavo de Carvalho's essay recounts a public debate against the Marxist historian Caio Prado Júnior at an anarchist centre but adds that the date escapes him; this project therefore records that debate as undated testimony and does not place it on any year.[6] [7] [3] [10]

São Paulo, SP, Brazil Pseudonymous debut with Editora Sagitário; the Logos courses — His first books appeared under pseudonyms with the Editora e Distribuidora Sagitário: Se a Esfinge Falasse (1946) and Realidade do Homem (1947) as Dan Andersen, Teses da Existência e da Inexistência de Deus (1946) as Charles Duclos. From 1947 he ran correspondence courses through his own Instituto Cultural Logos — a claim carried by a single tertiary source, the Portuguese Wikipedia, and flagged as such. He later put the pseudonymous output nobody credited to him at around twenty volumes.[12] [1] [3]

1954 1954 ? São Paulo, SP, Brazil O Homem que Nasceu Póstumo and Análise Dialética do Marxismo — Two books in one year: O Homem que Nasceu Póstumo — a book, distinct from the 1945 preface essay “O homem que foi um campo de batalha”, with which catalogues and search results routinely conflate it — and Análise Dialética do Marxismo, published while he was a regular presence in an anarchist milieu that argued with Marxists on its own terms.[12] [1]

Both readings are recorded and neither is adopted.[12] [1]
1957 1957 ? São Paulo, SP, Brazil Filosofia Concreta, the Minimax collection and the logic works — Filosofia Concreta — the book that names his system — is dated 1957 by the family-derived catalogue and 1956 by the Instituto Cultural Logos's own biography page, so the admirer site contradicts itself about the central title of the work it exists to promote. A fourth revised edition of Filosofia Concreta, in three volumes, appeared in 1961.[12] [13] [1]

Parallel storylines

The chronology read as parallel storylines: one row per lane, one column per decade, each cell the number of that lane’s events in that decade. An event that belongs to several storylines is counted in each, so the rows sum to more than the number of events. Select a number to jump to that decade in the chronology.

These four lanes are an editorial reading of this chronology, not a neutral fact about it. They were chosen because the sources themselves separate along these seams — a biography carried almost entirely by family-derived documents, a militancy documented by anarchist-studies scholarship, a bibliography that only a family and admirer catalogue lists in full, and a reception that is dated, partisan and disputed. Events sit in more than one lane whenever they belong to more than one story, so the lane totals add up to more than the number of events. Deliberately NOT given lanes: his philosophical doctrines themselves (this is a chronology of a life and its documents, not an exposition of concrete philosophy); the translations he made for other people's books, which are recorded inside events rather than tracked as a storyline; and the living admirer ecosystem around his work, which is a distinct subject. Absence from a lane is not a claim of irrelevance.

  3.   4. Reception & revival — The dated record of who read him and when: Vita's 1953 review and 1968 obituary (known only through quotation), Ladusãns's 1968 declaration, Jorge Jaime's 2000 chapter, the academic dissertations from 1988 onward, and Olavo de Carvalho's dated interventions between 1983 and 2020. Whether that last vector served the philosophy well is itself disputed, and this lane holds both sides of the dispute without settling it.[10] [3] [11] [14]

Key figures

Francisco Dias Ferreira dos Santos

Portugal / Brazil

Father. Portuguese, from Porto; a godson of Camilo Castelo Branco; actor, theatre impresario and a pioneer of cinema in Brazil (Guarany Filmes, Pelotas; O Crime dos Banhados, 1914). A Freemason and an anticlerical who nonetheless placed his son with the Jesuits. His son's own published description of him was edited between versions: the 1976 Loyola text drops “Grão-mestre da Maçonaria”.[3] [1]

Yolanda Duro Lhullier dos Santos

Brazil

Wife; married in 1929. The marriage and the household belong to the family-sourced cluster of the record — they are documented through the daughters' Monografia and the biographies built on it, not through independent civil records reached by this project.[3] [1]

Yolanda Lhullier dos Santos and Nadiejda Santos Nunes Galvão

Brazil

Daughters, and co-authors of the Monografia sobre Mário Ferreira dos Santos (2001) — the family document that nearly all the biographical detail on this site passes through, including Ladusãns's declaration and the catalogue of works. Nadiejda also gave a filmed testimony about her father, which this project has listed but not mined.[3] [21] [12]

Jaime Cubero

1926–1998 · Brazil

Anarchist militant of the Centro de Cultura Social in São Paulo, where the two met from 1945. In a 1997 interview he called Mário Ferreira dos Santos his great guru and the most cultured person he had known, and gave the highest of the circulating figures for the books sold — more than two million copies. Both are a witness's statements and are attributed as such here.[6] [7]

Pe. Stanislavs Ladusãns SJ

1912–1993 · Latvia / Brazil

Latvian-born Jesuit philosopher in Brazil. His declaration of 18 December 1968 — calling the subject a thinker of extraordinary value and a “Filósofo solitário” — fixes the birth and death dates used here, and he sent the works to faculty libraries in Europe. He also organized Rumos da Filosofia Atual no Brasil (Loyola, 1976), where the subject's self-presentation was published posthumously.[3]

Luís Washington Vita

Brazil

Philosopher and historian of Brazilian philosophy; the documented establishment reaction, twice — a dismissive review of Filosofia e Cosmovisão in 1953 and a tepid obituary in 1968. Both texts are known to this project only through quotation in the secondary literature; the Revista Brasileira de Filosofia originals were not reached, so what he actually argued beyond the quoted lines is unverified here.[3]

Jorge Jaime

Brazil

Historian of Brazilian philosophy. In the second volume of his História da Filosofia no Brasil (2000) he gave the subject a chapter, called him “o portentoso criador da filosofia concreta”, and wrote that a Brazilian philosopher had been condemned to silence throughout his life. That framing of the reception is his, and is recorded here as his.[3]

Olavo de Carvalho

1947–2022 · Brazil / USA

The reception vector, and a dated one: first encounter 1983, O Futuro do Pensamento Brasileiro and the Seminário lectures in 1997, the É Realizações edition of A Sabedoria das Leis Eternas in 2001, the Dicta&Contradicta essay, a five-lesson course in 2017, and the Guia para o estudo de sua obra in 2020. He described himself as the first Brazilian scholar to take the works out of ostracism; Pachêco, an academic, credits him with the structural map through which anyone with an overview of the philosophy has it. Gazeta do Povo reported in 2018 that he had been replaced as editor of the É Realizações reedition. He is the source of the 52-volume figure and of the “absolute isolation” framing, both attributed on this site and neither adopted.[10] [3] [11] [18]

Edson Manoel de Oliveira Filho

Brazil

Founder of É Realizações (1995), the publisher of the revival editions from 2001 — A Sabedoria das Leis Eternas (2001), the single-volume Filosofia Concreta (2009), Homens da Tarde (2019).[11] [16]

Elvis Amsterdã do Nascimento Pachêco

Brazil

Author of the 2019 UFSC dissertation, the fullest verified biography this project reached, and of Vida e Obra de Mário Ferreira dos Santos (Danúbio, 2023). He routes his reading through Olavo de Carvalho's structural map explicitly and says so.[3] [8]

Paulo H. F. S. F. Braga

Brazil / Portugal

Author of the 2024 study in Revista Ítaca 40 (UFRJ) and of a 2024 review in the Revista Filosófica de Coimbra — an academic reading of the anarchist milieu and of the publishing enterprise that does not route through the revival's framing.[7] [8]

Disambiguation & nuance

The confusions below are the ones that actually recur in catalogues, search results and secondary writing about this subject. Each records the distinction the sources themselves make, with the claims attributed to whoever makes them.

The Enciclopédia's volume count is contested — and no source supports “100+”

Four figures circulate, and each has an author: 52 (Olavo de Carvalho, in the Dicta&Contradicta essay), 45 (English Wikipedia), “cerca de 50” (Moura & Azevêdo, Problemata, 2015), and a family-derived catalogue numbering I–XLVIII that includes unpublished manuscripts and fragments. They are not counting the same thing — multi-volume titles, unpublished material and planned-but-unwritten volumes each move the total — which is why this site attributes every figure and adopts none. One further point is a correction rather than an attribution: the claim of a “100+ volume plan”, which appears in popular writing about him, is supported by no source this project reached, and nothing on this site asserts one.[10] [2] [5] [12]

“Silenced” or “independent”? — two framings, both attributed

One reading holds that he was marginalized: Jorge Jaime wrote in 2000 that a Brazilian philosopher had been condemned to silence throughout his life and called the criticism of him “facciosa”; Ladusãns called him a solitary philosopher in 1968; Olavo de Carvalho describes a Vico-like absolute isolation and a wall of silence after his death, floats a possible PCB contribution to it after the Caio Prado Júnior debate, and then concludes against his own hypothesis — Brazil ignored the philosopher, he writes, simply because it did not know what he was talking about. The other reading holds that much of the distance was chosen: Pachêco notes that he was obstinate in creating his own position and in refusing public office, though he did accept the late Jesuit teaching post; a man selling books by the hundreds of thousands door to door is not straightforwardly an ignored man; and Olavo himself concedes that the books' omissions, truncated sentences and general proofing errors made them a torment to read — an explanation, in his phrase, that explains without justifying. This site records both framings with their authors and adjudicates neither.[10] [3] [7]

“O homem que foi um campo de batalha” is an essay, not a book

Written in 1943 and published in 1945 as the preface to his translation of Nietzsche's The Will to Power for the Livraria do Globo. It is not a separate volume, and it is not O Homem que Nasceu Póstumo (1954), which is a book and a different text. Listings that turn the essay into a title, or merge the two, are wrong on both counts.[3] [12]

institutoculturallogos.com is an admirer site, not an estate — and no estate site is confirmed

The present-day Instituto Cultural Logos website carries the fullest work catalogue, a biography and a finding aid of 236-plus studies, all of it valuable and all of it a family and admirer source, labeled as such wherever this dataset cites it. It is not the Instituto Cultural Logos he himself ran from 1947, and it is not an estate. Nor is there another: the domains marioferreiradossantos.com and .com.br reset when tried and are absent from search indexes, so no official estate site is confirmed to exist. Do not cite one.[12] [13] [14]

The revival is disputed by the people inside it

Olavo de Carvalho organized the 2001 É Realizações edition and is credited by Pachêco — an academic, not a partisan — as the one to whom anyone with an overall view of this philosophy owes it. Gazeta do Povo reported in 2018 that he had been replaced as editor of the reedition and that its editorial treatment had changed; that is the column's account, recorded as such. É Realizações published a “Nota” about Olavo de Carvalho and the edition of the works, which this project could not read because the upload sits behind a bot wall — so its position is not characterized here, in any direction. Whether the Olavo-driven reading serves the philosophy is itself contested in the scholarship: Pachêco routes through his structural map explicitly, Braga's 2024 Ítaca article does not.[11] [3] [10] [7]

Not in the DHBB, and not the other Ferreira dos Santos

The CPDOC/FGV Dicionário Histórico-Biográfico Brasileiro has no entry for the philosopher. That is a checked negative, not an untried assumption: the search was run over the repository and returned only an unrelated “Ferreira dos Santos” born in 1894, which doubles as the positive control that the search itself worked. The surname string is common in Brazilian sources and pulls in unrelated people; a bare “Ferreira dos Santos” hit is not evidence about this subject.[20]
